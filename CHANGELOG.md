# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Distance and the route between two places** (MODULES-679). `route` on `<chikyu-earth>`, `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` and `<chikyu-space>` (absent by default; the property is the two places or `null`) draws the line between them. `routeInfo` gives the WGS84 geodesic (`kilometres`, Vincenty), the great-circle distance on a sphere of 6371.0088 km, the initial and final bearings, and a flight time at 900 km/h. The line is split at the date line. Nothing is drawn again at rest. There is no flat world map in this package: the line globe is the flat drawing, and `routeRuns` is there for a page that draws its own.

## 0.42.0 - 2026-11-12

- **Bodies are made only when they are big enough to see** (MODULES-1192). `<chikyu-space>` made the painter's drawing of the Sun, the Moon and every planet in every view, with their mapped pictures, however small or far from the view. It now asks the painter for a body only when the body is in the view and at least 6 CSS pixels across its radius (`BODY_MAKE_PX`); a smaller one, or one out of the view, is a plain sphere of the right colour (the Moon is the view's own Moon), and a body that has shrunk below 3 pixels or left the view is let go of its pictures after four seconds (`BODY_KEEP_MS`), so one that comes and goes is not made again and again. Measured in the default Earth view (Nov 10, centred on -100,40): the pictures loaded went from 10 (the Sun, the Moon and the eight planets, 150,446 bytes) to 1 (the Moon, 16,974 bytes); `stats.bodies.mapped` says how many drawings are the painter's. wakusei-planets' `credit` therefore narrows by itself: an Earth view with no CC BY body in it shows no CC BY line, Jupiter adds Nestaeric's credit, the Sun adds Solar System Scope's, and no line is written while the painter holds nothing. Nothing is drawn at rest and the GPU objects are flat across moves. Tests: `e2e/space-bodies.gl.ts`, `e2e/space-credit.gl.ts`, `e2e/space-full.gl.ts`.

## 0.41.0 - 2026-11-12

- **The credit under the space view follows the painter's credit** (MODULES-1158). wakusei-planets' painter (0.39 and later) carries `credit: { en, ja }` for the pictures of the bodies the view holds, and the view makes the Sun, the Moon and every planet in each view, so the line names all their pictures in an Earth view as in a Jupiter view (more than is big on the screen is safe, less would be the fault); so `<chikyu-space>` reads it again whenever a body drawing asks for a redraw (a picture arrived, a window of tiles changed, a body was made) and writes the line only when its text changed, never at every frame; no credit, no line. Pass the painter as it is: `{ ...painter }` reads `credit` once. A page that writes the credit itself (`bodies-credit="off"`) is sent `chikyu-bodies-credit` when it changed, with `detail` `{ en, ja }` or `null`. The demo drops its own `PLANET_CREDIT` and uses the painter's; its full-screen page follows the event. Tests: `e2e/space-credit.gl.ts` (a painter of the test's own; and wakusei-planets' own, wrapped to log the bodies made and the reads of its credit after they are made: every body is made, the Sun's and Jupiter's CC BY 4.0 credits are in the line, and it is the painter's credit as it is now). Lazy making of the bodies out of view, which would narrow the credit, is a separate ticket. The credit line is at most four lines high and scrolls: with every body made, wakusei-planets' credit is some twenty lines and covered the whole view (it hid the ground from the drag, air and follow tests in CI).

## 0.40.0 - 2026-11-12

- **Names of different layers never overlap** (MODULES-1072). On `<chikyu-space>` and `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` each layer placed its names alone: city names ignored the places, the markers' labels ignored the places' names, and star names ignored every layer's names and rings, so a name could sit over another layer's. The layers now lay out in a fixed order, each keeping clear of the boxes of those before it: the places' names, rings and marks with the ringed satellites, then the markers' labels, then the cities' names, then the stars' and constellations' names (the countries' names, which already kept clear of the others, come after the cities'). A star whose name box or ring touches an earlier box is left out and the next star is named. The order is the draw's: the realistic Earth's places are laid out before its markers and the sky after the cities, as the space view's sky is after its cities. `OverlayHost` has an optional `taken()` (the places' boxes, which `attachMarkers` reads), `CityLayoutOptions` has `taken`, and a satellite's ring is among the boxes the places take. Boxes are handed over when names are laid out, which is when the view changes; nothing is laid out or drawn at rest. Tests: `test/label-overlap.test.ts` and `e2e/label-overlap.gl.ts`.
- **A glide is counted on the page, not polled** (MODULES-1190). `e2e/ride-above.gl.ts` watched for the camera's glide into the above view by polling `stats.ride.gliding`, and a short glide could be missed (seen in CI on 2026-11-12). The page now counts each glide at every animation frame and the test reads the count; it fails when a view change never glides. A test change only; the package is unchanged.

## 0.39.0 - 2026-11-12

- **The painted trail follows the orbit at any clock, and costs nothing until it is used** (MODULES-1188). A month laid down at once, or a fast clock, took samples too far apart and drew chords across the map: samples are now at most a third of a trip apart. A clock run backwards laid down nothing for the older end, so the trail shrank: it now fills it in. A coverage swath over the date line was cut at the edge of the map: it now wraps. Switching to a satellite that paints nothing left the old picture on the globe: the cleared picture is sent. The trail's two megabytes and its picture on the graphics card were made in every view, painting or not: they are made when the first satellite is painted, and `clearPaint()` gives them back.
- **Sharp ground when riding above a satellite, and the model there** (MODULES-1184). `<chikyu-space>` takes `tiles` (attribute and property, as `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` does) and lays the finer map tiles over its Earth: the level is chosen from the pixels a unit of ground takes under the camera, so from 150 km above the ISS the ground is the finest level (4) and from 3,000 km it is level 3, where it was one blurred 4096 picture; only the tiles under the camera are loaded (at most 48, freed after four seconds), a view of the whole Earth asks for none, and nothing is drawn at rest. `stats.tiles` has the tile set's state and `chikyu-tiles` is sent once when a set has been read. `TileCamera` has an optional `perspective` (`TilePerspective`, new in `/realistic`) for a camera with a real place, which `tilesInView` and `pointOnEarth` use in place of the disc; a disc is read as before. The satellite's model (`setSatellitePainter`) was already drawn in the `above` view as in `follow`; the tests now say so at 150 km (`e2e/ride-above-model.gl.ts`, `e2e/ride-above-tiles.gl.ts`). The demo's space view uses its tiles.
- **A press on a named star or a constellation figure tells the page** (MODULES-1179). `chikyu-sky-pick` is sent on `<chikyu-space>` and `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` when a reader presses or taps one. A star's detail is `{ kind: "star", hr, name, designation, x, y, pointerType }` (`name` as drawn, `x` and `y` CSS pixels inside the element, `pointerType` `mouse`, `pen`, `touch` or `keyboard`). A figure's detail is `{ kind: "constellation", abbreviation, pointerType }`. Each named star and each figure in view is a button, so a keyboard reaches it and Enter picks it, and the pick is announced; the keys `C` and `Shift`+`C` (the next and the previous figure) send it too (`pointerType` `keyboard`). Nothing is drawn at rest.
- **A build without the sky cultures** (MODULES-1185). `@johnmorrisdotca/chikyu-earth/space/no-cultures` and `@johnmorrisdotca/chikyu-earth/realistic/no-cultures` are `<chikyu-space>` and `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` with no sky culture but the IAU's: nothing under `hoshi-stars/cultures` is imported, so a site's bundler no longer needs a stub for it, and `constellation-culture` is `iau` whatever a page sets (one warning). The default entries are unchanged: they bring in `western`, `japanese` and `chinese`, a dynamic import each. How: the elements' code moved to `element-base.ts` in each folder; `space/element` and `realistic/element` are now that code plus `sky/cultures.ts`, which sets the table the sky layer reads (`setCultureLoaders`). A test reads each entry's imports and finds no `hoshi-stars/cultures` in the two new ones and all three in the default ones, and the usage test bundles the new entries with every such import refused.
- **The demo takes every planet picture at the widest width again** (MODULES-1175). The demo's `wakusei-planets` is `^0.38.0`, which stops at the widest picture its package ships when a page gives a `textureBase` and no `farBase`, so the demo's cap of 1024 on the painter (and its 404s for 2048) is gone.

## 0.38.0 - 2026-11-12

- **Paint a satellite's path on the Earth** (MODULES-1167). `paint` on `<chikyu-space>` and `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` is `{ id, length, swath }`. `id` is a NORAD catalogue number or `group:index`. `length` is `1d` (the default), `1w` or `1m` of simulated time. `swath` is `track` (the point under the satellite, the default) or `coverage` (the ground where the satellite is at least 10° above the horizon, from its height). `clearPaint()` takes the trail off. Changing the satellite clears it. The trail is one equirectangular texture on the globe. It is stamped only while simulated time moves, one upload a frame, and nothing at rest or while painting is off. A part older than `length` fades out. The date line and the poles are split so the line does not cross the map. The demo's satellite card has Paint, Clear, and 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.
- **No hitch from the once-a-second satellite pin** (MODULES-1171). Above 60 times real time each satellite's place is placed by SGP4 about once a second; that ran for the whole group in one frame (about 1.6 ms for 2,251 satellites, about 12 ms for 17,000). It is now spread over the frames: a slice of the group each frame, in proportion to the time the frame took (a thirtieth at 30 frames a second), each satellite turned from its own pin time, so every satellite is still pinned about once a second and no frame runs SGP4 for the whole group. A jump of the clock (a date typed in, a scrub) still pins the whole group at once, so a place is never turned across days. `Satellites.pin(ms, first?, count?)` takes a range (the whole group by default, as before) and keeps each satellite's pin time; an eccentric orbit, which `arc` places by SGP4 itself, is no longer pinned too. Measured on the update of a satellite group at a steady 60 and 30 frames a second, 17,000 made-up satellites, three runs each, before and after: at 600 times the 99th percentile of the update fell from 8.6 ms to 4.4 to 5.2 ms and the longest from 12.7 to 15.5 ms to 6.3 to 8.4 ms; at 3600 times from 8.4 to 9.0 ms to 5.9 to 6.1 ms and from 9.5 to 17.8 ms to 6.4 to 7.9 ms. The 90th percentile rose by about 0.5 to 0.8 ms (2.3 to 3.8 ms to 3.1 to 4.4 ms), since the same work is now done a little every frame. The demo's own catalogue, all 14 groups, is 4,084 satellites.
- **Place dots cost less, and a missing ring is told only when a place is left out** (MODULES-1165). The fallback circles (when the three.js parts have no `Points`) no longer ask for a compositor layer each, skip a move that did not change, and are projected once a frame. They fade at the Earth's edge and are dimmer at night, as the rings in the picture are. `stats.places.sprites` counts only the rings in the one draw call (0 in the fallback); `stats.places.dots` counts the circles drawn in the page instead. `chikyu-places-unavailable` is sent once, and only when places in sight had no room (`omitted`), not merely because `Points` is missing; its `detail.reason` names `PLACE_DOTS` and `detail.omitted` says how many places had no room. A browser test checks that `place-groups` and `placeGroups` turn the gathering off and on as each other's mirror, in both views.
- **The passes page's world map has half the points** (MODULES-807). Its 720 by 360 map drew all 10,000 points of the country outlines, more than it can show, and in Chromium drawing on a software GPU the map held up the page's frames after it opened. Each ring is now thinned in the browser to within half a map unit of the original (Douglas-Peucker, less than a pixel at the size the map is shown), and the path is written to one decimal: about 5,300 points and 58 KB, from 10,371 and 112 KB. Measured on the Mac in Chromium with SwiftShader and GPU rasterization (three opens each, the longest time with no frame after the page opened): desk 1.4, 2.2 and 0.36 s with the full outline, 0.29 s each with the thinned one, 0.16 to 0.19 s with no map at all; phone 0.43 s once with the full outline, under 0.07 s otherwise. NOTICE.md says the map is a derived drawing of Natural Earth's outlines.

## 0.37.0 - 2026-11-12

- **A satellite's owner country as an ISO code** (MODULES-1112), for a page that puts a flag on a satellite card. `satelliteInfo` and the click event's `info` have `ownerCountry`, an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code (`CN`, `US`, `PK`), when the elements carry an owner code (`COUNTRY_CODE` or `OWNER`) and the owner is one country. An organisation (`ESA`, `ISS`, `ITSO`, `SES`, `GLOB`, `EUME`, `NATO`, `AB` and the like), several countries, an unknown owner, or a code the list does not give to one country (`CIS`, the former Soviet registration) has none, and the field is left off. New in `@johnmorrisdotca/chikyu-earth/satellites` and `/catalogue`: `ownerCountry(code)` and `OWNER_COUNTRIES`, every code of the catalogue's owner list, each row checked. Data only: no flag, no new dependency.
- **City names can be taken off and put back, and a font is measured once** (MODULES-1097). Removing `<chikyu-space>` or `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` while a tier of city names is still loading no longer leaves a second canvas and a second names box when it is put back: a load that arrives after the layer is gone does nothing, and neither does its error. The font used to measure the names is read again only when the style, the size or the language changes, not on every draw. A font that finishes loading after the space view has measured its body-name boxes is measured again.
- **Satellites stay on the picture when the clock runs faster than a minute a second** (MODULES-860). Above 60 times real time they were hidden, because two SGP4 samples would be too far apart to interpolate. Each satellite is now a short streak along its orbit for the stretch one frame spans: one SGP4 sample about once a second, then turned by its mean motion, on `<chikyu-space>` and `<chikyu-earth-realistic>`. `satellite-tint="orbit"` (off by default) colours them by orbit class. Dots grow as the Earth is drawn larger and shrink as it is drawn smaller, and a dark rim keeps a pale dot readable on the bright ground. `stats.satellites.placed` is how many are drawn, `segments` how many streaks, and `epochMs` the newest element set's moment. New on `Satellites`: `pin`, `arc`, `classColour` and `newestEpochMs`; `tooFast` stays true above 60 times, so a page that read it as "hidden" should read `placed` or `segments` instead. **A page that hands over its own three.js parts (`setThreeLoader`) should add `LineSegments`** for the streaks (the demo's `three-subset.ts` has it); without it the satellites above 60 times are dots alone, with one console warning, and nothing else changes. An orbit with an eccentricity of 0.02 or more (a Molniya, a transfer orbit) is placed by SGP4 itself on every frame there, because turning it at the mean motion put it kilometres off its path.
- **Face a way again while riding** (MODULES-1105). `face("ahead" | "behind" | "left" | "right" | "down" | "up")` turns the camera there in half a second (at once when the reader asked for less motion) and keeps the zoom. Ahead is along the way it goes, behind is the opposite, left and right are across that way, down is straight at the Earth and up is straight away from it. In the above view, down looks straight down at the satellite. `facing` is that way until the view is turned or tipped, and `chikyu-ride-face` is sent when it changes. Keys, while the element is focused: W, S, A, D, R and F. The arrow keys stay with the view controls. The demo has a Face row and the address keeps `face`.
- **Facing, made safe** (MODULES-1105 review): following a planet or the Moon there is no way to face, so `face()` answers `false` and nothing is told; a held key's repeats do not start the glide again; a key typed in a field inside the element, or in editable text, is left to it.

- **The grid and country names on the Earth** (MODULES-1150). `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` and `<chikyu-space>` take `grid` (the property `grid`) and draw the meridians and parallels on the Earth: every 30 degrees while its radius on the screen is under 220 CSS pixels (`GRID_FINE_RADIUS`), every 15 above; the equator brightest, the tropics and polar circles bright (the IAU's tilt, `EARTH_TILT`), the prime meridian warm; under the clouds, light by day and dim by night, turning with the Earth. One batch of lines (one draw call; 3,240 segments, a 95 kB buffer, made once). Each element's code grows by 3.7 to 3.9 kB gzipped; nothing is drawn at rest. `stats.grid` and `stats.countryNames` have the counts; the pure parts (`gridLines`, `countrySpots`, `layoutCountryNames` and the rest) are in `@johnmorrisdotca/chikyu-earth/realistic`. The demo has Grid and Country names buttons (`grid=on`, `country-names=on` in the address). New guide: The grid and country names on the Earth (English and Japanese).

The first `PLACE_MARKS` (32) in sight, `here` first and then in the order given, stand up out of the ground with their names as before; every other place is a ring lying on the ground, all of them one draw call (three.js `Points`, made only while a ring is shown, its buffers written again only when the places or their groups change), so a thousand places add one draw call to a frame and nothing at rest. A place may have a colour of its own (`color`: a hex colour, `rgb()`/`hsl()` with numbers, or a colour's name). Places on the night side are drawn at half strength and their names a little dimmer, still readable (`layoutPlaces` takes `sun` and each shape says its `night`); nothing is dimmed with `light="lit"`. `goToPlace(idOrLabel, { smooth })` turns the view so a place is in the middle, at a pace a reader can follow, or at once for a reader who asked for less motion; in the space view only while the camera looks at the Earth. `stats.places` adds `marks`, `sprites` and `groups`. A click on the globe or on a place is not part of this release: it waits for a decision (MODULES-1082).
- **Borders on the Earth** (MODULES-864). `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` and `<chikyu-space>` take `borders` (`countries`, `regions`, or bare for both; the property `borders` as `{ countries, regions }`) and draw the borders on the land as thin lines that turn with the Earth, above the clouds and under the air, by day and by night. Each set is one batch of lines (one draw call); the 3,012 country segments are a 72 kB buffer; the element's code grows by 1.9 kB gzipped; nothing is drawn at rest. `chikyu-borders-unavailable` is sent when the shapes cannot be loaded, and the Earth goes on without them; `stats.borders` has the counts. ## 0.35.0 - 2026-11-12

- **The figures follow a sky culture** (MODULES-1125). `constellation-culture` is `iau` (the 88, the default), `western`, `japanese` (the 88 by their Japanese names, then the traditional figures such as Subaru, the Pleiades) or `chinese`; the lines, the hovered and picked figure and its name follow it, and the events carry `id`, `kind` and `culture`. The value is exactly what the page sets (a Japanese page keeps the IAU's figures unless it says `japanese`); an unknown value is `iau` with one warning. Each culture's data is a dynamic import of its own, fetched only when asked for. hoshi-stars' `createFigureLines` takes no culture yet, so the lines of a culture are laid out here from `figures(culture)`.
- **Say which constellation was picked, reveal its name on hover, and an option to name every constellation** (MODULES-1108). A mouse resting on a figure (its lines, or inside it) lights it and fades its name in at the middle of the part of it that is on the picture, inside the frame, off the Earth and clear of the star names; a click or tap picks it and the name stays until another pick or a click on empty sky, `Escape` or the property set to `null` (`C` and `Shift`+`C` pick with the keyboard). `chikyu-constellation-hover` and `chikyu-constellation-pick` are sent with `{ abbreviation, name, english, japanese, culture }` (`null` when let go), and `constellation` reads or sets the pick. The name sat above the figure's whole box, which includes the part off the picture, so a figure that ran off the top had its name left near its bottom. `constellation-names` (off by default) writes every figure's name in view, quieter than the star names, in the page's language, with no overlap. The hit test runs once a frame at most, only after the pointer has moved, and the layer is drawn again only when the hovered figure changes. A pick is read out by a visually hidden polite live region (a hover is not). A pick set before a sky culture has arrived is kept until it has.
- **A page can mark places where the sky's names must not go** (MODULES-1124). `<chikyu-space>` and `<chikyu-earth-realistic>` take `keepClear` (a list of rectangles in the element's pixels, or of page elements) and the attribute `keep-clear` (a CSS selector). A star name that touches one is left out, a star whose point is in one is not named, and the picked constellation's name moves clear of it; the lines are still drawn. The boxes are read when the list changes or the page is resized or scrolled (the names are laid out again at the next frame, once however many events), never at each frame.

## 0.6.0 - 2026-11-02 (never published on its own: it ships in 0.7.0)

The Sun, the Moon and the planets: `<chikyu-space>`, an optional entry point, under the same performance budget as the Earth from space.

- **`<chikyu-space>`** (`@johnmorrisdotca/chikyu-earth/space/element`) places the Earth, the Moon, the Sun and the planets (Mercury to Neptune) in their positions for a date and time, with the Moon's
  phase and the Earth's sunlit side coming out of the light (the Moon is half lit on 2 November 2026, last quarter). Positions are computed in the browser by **astronomy-engine** (MIT, a new optional peer dependency;
  nothing is fetched). Time controls: `time` (an ISO date or `now`), `speed` (1 real time, 0 held, 3600 an hour a second, 86400 a day a second, negative backwards). Zoom from the Earth out to the solar system and back with
  the shared controls (`zoom-min`, `zoom-max`), and `zoomFor("moon" | "sun" | "inner" | "solar-system" | "earth")` for the jumps. Names beside the bodies and the planets' orbits can be switched off.
- **Two scales, and it says which**: `compressed` (default; every direction real, the Moon 10 Earth radii away instead of 60, the Sun 44, the planets round the Sun at 44 radii times their distance in astronomical
  units to the power 0.45, sizes enlarged) and `true` (real distances and sizes, where the planets are specks). The demo says "Compressed scale: ..." whenever it is on. [docs/space.md](docs/space.md).
- **Budget, each line tested** (`e2e/space.gl.ts`): paused, 0 draw calls and 0 frame requests in 4 s; real time, 0 draw calls in 6 s (a draw a minute); a day a second: 1.00 days a second of game time, 29.4 draws a second,
  and 2.0 ephemeris lookups a second (the ephemeris is looked up once a game-second span and interpolated, within 0.1 degree of the Moon's true place); nothing drawn out of sight at a day a second; the textures follow the zoom and
  are freed after it; removal frees what it held; a lost context is recovered. The space soak (10 minutes, 945 steps from the Earth to the solar system and back, at every speed, both scales, orbits and names toggled): JS heap 8.05 to 8.45 MB after garbage collection (flat from the fifth minute), GPU objects 46 to 46, textures, buffers and programs equal to the start, the average 76 GL draw calls a second (about 25 a frame, so 3 frames a second), one WebGL context, no error.
- **Pictures**: the Moon (NASA SVS 4720, LROC colour mosaic, 1024 and 2048 wide, 306 KB) joins the Earth's; the package's textures are 1.85 MB. The planets are plain colours.
- **Shared parts**: the texture loader (`src/realistic/textures.ts`) and the page's one renderer (`src/realistic/shared.ts`) now serve both the Earth from space and the space view, so a page keeps one WebGL context between them. The
  Earth's shaders look at the camera instead of assuming one, so they work from any angle.
- **Research, no code** ([docs/satellite-sources.md](docs/satellite-sources.md), MODULES-85): CelesTrak (grey), Space-Track (red for a package), the r/SpaceX API (archived, offline), Launch Library 2 (grey), Wikipedia and Wikidata
  (green) for launches; the recommendation is a satellite view that takes elements the page supplies. John chooses before MODULES-86 and MODULES-87 start.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-11-01

The first scaffold, and the first working globe.

- `globe()` draws an orthographic globe as an SVG string: an ocean disc, the country outlines and the rim,
  in two line styles (`line`, `thick`), with an optional graticule.
- `<chikyu-earth>` puts that globe on a page as a web component. It opens centred on where you are
  (the `center` attribute wins over geolocation) and turns at the Earth's own rate — one sidereal day,
  23 h 56 min 4.0905 s, per revolution.
- Country outlines are Natural Earth's, baked into the package at build time; each carries its English and
  Japanese name and ISO alpha-2 code from kuni. d3-geo does the projection, the only runtime dependency.
- A demo (`pnpm dev`), unit tests, a package check and a Playwright demo test, with CI to run them.
